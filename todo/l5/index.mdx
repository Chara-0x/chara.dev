## Recap: linear combination & span

A **linear combination** of vectors $\{\vec v_1,\dots,\vec v_k\}$ is
$$
c_1\vec v_1+\cdots+c_k\vec v_k.
$$
The **span** of $\{\vec v_1,\dots,\vec v_k\}$ is the set of **all** such combinations.

---

## Matrix–vector multiplication

<Callout variant="definition" title="Definition (Matrix–vector multiplication)">
Given column vectors $\vec a_1,\dots,\vec a_k\in\mathbb{R}^m$ and scalars $x_1,\dots,x_k$,
$$
x_1\vec a_1+\cdots+x_k\vec a_k
\quad\text{is abbreviated}\quad
A\vec x,
$$
where
$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}\vec a_1&\vec a_2&\cdots&\vec a_k\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k},
\qquad
\vec x=\begin{bmatrix}x_1\\x_2\\\vdots\\x_k\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}.
$$
Thus $A\vec x\in\mathbb{R}^m$ is the linear combination of the columns of $A$ with weights given by $\vec x$.
</Callout>

**Sanity check (row·column):**
$$
\begin{bmatrix}1&-2&3&-4\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}7\\3\\1\\2\end{bmatrix}
=1\cdot7+(-2)\cdot3+3\cdot1+(-4)\cdot2
=7-6+3-8=-4.
$$

**Abbreviation link.** The vector equation
$$
x_1\vec a_1+\cdots+x_k\vec a_k=\vec b
$$
is the **matrix equation**
$$
A\vec x=\vec b.
$$

---

## How to solve $A\vec x=\vec b$

Write the augmented matrix and row-reduce.

**Augmented matrix:**
$$
\left[
\begin{array}{cccc|c}
a_{11}&a_{12}&\cdots&a_{1n}&b_1\\
a_{21}&a_{22}&\cdots&a_{2n}&b_2\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
a_{m1}&a_{m2}&\cdots&a_{mn}&b_m
\end{array}
\right]
$$

Reduce to REF/RREF, then read off the solution(s).

*Advanced note.* If RREF has $k$ free variables, then the solution set (when nonempty) is a $k$-dimensional affine set:
$$
\vec x\in \vec p+\operatorname{Span}\{\vec v_1,\dots,\vec v_k\}.
$$

---

## Spanning the whole space

Another paraphrase:

$$
\vec b\in\operatorname{Span}\{\vec a_1,\dots,\vec a_n\}
\iff
A\vec x=\vec b\ \text{is solvable}.
$$

In particular, the columns of $A$ span $\mathbb{R}^m$ **iff** for **every** $\vec b\in\mathbb{R}^m$ there exists $\vec x$ with $A\vec x=\vec b$ (i.e., the system is solvable for all right-hand sides).

<Callout variant="theorem" title="Theorem (Four equivalent conditions)">
The following are equivalent for an $m\times n$ matrix $A$.

a. The column vectors of $A$ span $\mathbb{R}^m$.  

b. Each $\vec b\in\mathbb{R}^m$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$.  

c. For each $\vec b\in\mathbb{R}^m$, the equation $A\vec x=\vec b$ has (at least) a solution.  

d. $A$ has a **pivot in every row**.
</Callout>

**Note.** It is the **coefficient matrix $A$** (not $[\;A\mid\vec b\;]$) that must have a pivot in each row for its columns to span $\mathbb{R}^m$.

## Example: membership in a span

Is
$$
\begin{bmatrix}6\\-5\end{bmatrix}\in
\operatorname{Span}
\left\{
\begin{bmatrix}2\\4\end{bmatrix},
\begin{bmatrix}3\\-2\end{bmatrix},
\begin{bmatrix}-1\\5\end{bmatrix}
\right\}?
$$

**Translate to $A\vec x=\vec b$:**
$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}2&3&-1\\[2pt]4&-2&5\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\vec b=\begin{bmatrix}6\\-5\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Row-reduce $[\;A\mid \vec b\;]$:
$$
\left[
\begin{array}{ccc|c}
2&3&-1&6\\
4&-2&5&-5
\end{array}
\right]
\ \xrightarrow{R_2\leftarrow R_2-2R_1}\
\left[
\begin{array}{ccc|c}
2&3&-1&6\\
0&-8&7&-17
\end{array}
\right].
$$

The last column is **not** a pivot $\Rightarrow$ the system is **consistent**. Therefore
$$
\begin{bmatrix}6\\-5\end{bmatrix}\in
\operatorname{Span}
\left\{
\begin{bmatrix}2\\4\end{bmatrix},
\begin{bmatrix}3\\-2\end{bmatrix},
\begin{bmatrix}-1\\5\end{bmatrix}
\right\}.
$$

**Do these three span $\mathbb{R}^2$?** Reduce $A$:
$$
\begin{bmatrix}2&3&-1\\4&-2&5\end{bmatrix}
\ \xrightarrow{R_2\leftarrow R_2-2R_1}\
\begin{bmatrix}2&3&-1\\0&-8&7\end{bmatrix},
$$
which has a **pivot in every row** $\Rightarrow$ the columns of $A$ span $\mathbb{R}^2$.

## Solution structure: particular + homogeneous

Any solution set of $A\vec x=\vec b$ can be decomposed as
$$
\vec x=\vec p+\vec x_h,
$$
where $\vec p$ is a **particular solution** ($A\vec p=\vec b$) and $\vec x_h$ runs over **all** solutions to the **homogeneous** system $A\vec x=\vec 0$.

If there are $r$ free variables, the homogeneous solutions form a subspace
$$
\vec x_h\in\operatorname{Span}\{\vec v_1,\dots,\vec v_r\},
$$
hence, when there are infinitely many solutions,
$$
\vec x=\vec p+\operatorname{Span}\{\vec v_1,\dots,\vec v_r\}.
$$

---

## Linearity of $A(\cdot)$

Matrix–vector multiplication respects linearity:
$$
A(\vec u+\vec v)=A\vec u+A\vec v,
\qquad
A(c\,\vec u)=c\,(A\vec u),
$$
for all $\vec u,\vec v\in\mathbb{R}^n$ and $c\in\mathbb{R}$. These identities justify viewing $A\vec x$ as a linear combination of the columns of $A$ with coefficients from $\vec x$.
